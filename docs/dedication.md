## Why this book

I do not approach this book as a leader who claims to know everything about healthcare AI. That posture would be misleading, because healthcare is too broad, too regulated, too human, and too complex for any single individual to fully possess. I see myself instead as one component within a vast, emergent, intelligent system that is greater than its individual parts. Healthcare includes patients, clinicians, caregivers, engineers, regulators, payers, researchers, manufacturers, operators, and many others. As generative and agentic AI enter this system, they do not replace that collective intelligence; they become another force that must be understood, governed, and integrated responsibly.

My perspective comes from being a technology practitioner. I have built systems, experimented with ideas, researched emerging methods, read across disciplines, and worked through practical constraints. I have also seen healthcare challenges from multiple angles: MedTech, pharma, payer, and provider environments each expose a different part of the same larger puzzle. Over time, I have learned that meaningful progress often depends on connecting dots across domains that are usually discussed separately.

This book is my attempt to crystallize what I have learned through that process. It is meant to pass forward knowledge acquired through research, reading, implementation, and experimentation, so that others can use it as a foundation. My hope is that people entering healthcare AI, including college students, early-career practitioners, and technology professionals moving into the domain, can find a clearer path through the complexity.

## How to read this book

This is **not** a textbook that serves every answer on a plate. Healthcare AI cannot be learned well through passive reading alone. The field is shaped by ambiguity, regulation, data quality, workflow constraints, clinical risk, business incentives, and human trust. To understand it, the reader must question, explore, test, and connect ideas.

The purpose of this book is therefore to provoke thought as much as to explain concepts. The practitioner sections, simplified examples, and conceptual frameworks are designed to create starting points. They are not presented as complete industrial implementations. Real-world healthcare algorithms, compliance engines, FHIR mappings, de-identification pipelines, and agentic workflows require deeper validation, domain review, privacy controls, governance, and operational discipline.

| Reader stance | What this book asks you to do |
|---|---|
| Do not only consume | Pause, question assumptions, and test whether an idea holds in a real healthcare context. |
| Do not memorize blindly | Understand why a method works, where it fails, and what constraints healthcare adds. |
| Do not treat examples as production systems | Use simplified code and rules as learning scaffolds, then investigate how industrial systems differ. |
| Do not wait for perfect understanding | Build small mental models, experiment responsibly, and refine them as your domain knowledge grows. |

This is also the method I use for my own learning. I read, experiment, observe, connect ideas, and then return to the material with better questions. Passive learning rarely creates durable understanding. Active inquiry does. If this book helps the reader become more curious, more careful, and more capable of contributing responsibly to healthcare AI, then it has served its purpose.
